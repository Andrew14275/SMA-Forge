01 — Concept

What Are Shape
Memory Alloys?

Smart metallic materials that can recover their original shape after large deformations — up to 8–10% strain — when exposed to temperature or stress changes. That's 10× greater than ordinary steel's elastic limit.

A Shape Memory Alloy (SMA) is a metallic alloy that, after being deformed at a low temperature, can return to its pre-deformed shape when heated above a critical temperature known as the austenite finish temperature (Af). This behaviour arises from a reversible solid-state phase transformation between two crystal structures — martensite and austenite.

The Core Phenomenon

Why Metals Can "Remember"

Ordinary metals deform permanently when bent past their elastic limit. SMAs are different — their internal crystal structure can switch between two stable phases depending on temperature and stress.

This transformation is not just elastic springback. The atoms literally reorganise themselves into a different arrangement, then reorganise back — carrying the material's shape information with them each time.

🌡️
Key Temperature: Af
For Nitinol used in biomedical devices, Af is tuned to just below 37°C — human body temperature — so the alloy is superelastic at body temp and activates automatically inside the body.
⚗️
It's not shape memory like rubber.
Rubber is elastic — it stores mechanical energy. SMAs store thermodynamic information in their crystal lattice. A completely different mechanism.

The Two Behaviours

Shape Memory Effect vs Superelasticity

SMAs exhibit two distinct behaviours depending on the temperature at which deformation occurs.

01
Effect One

Shape Memory Effect

The alloy is deformed while cold (in the martensite phase). When heated above Af, it snaps back to its original shape — seemingly defying permanent deformation.

Cool below Mf → martensite phase
Apply stress → deformation occurs
Remove stress → shape stays deformed
Heat above Af → original shape returns
02
Effect Two

Superelasticity

Above Af, the alloy can be deformed up to 8% strain and will spring back without any heating once the stress is removed. Used in orthodontic wires and stents.

Temperature above Af → austenite phase
Apply stress → stress-induced martensite
Remove stress → reverts to austenite
Shape fully recovers — no heating needed

The Most Common SMA

Nitinol — NiTi Alloy

Nitinol (Nickel-Titanium Naval Ordnance Laboratory) is the most commercially significant SMA. Discovered in 1962 by William Buehler and Frederick Wang, it consists of nearly equal atomic percentages of nickel and titanium.

The exact ratio can be tuned to adjust the transformation temperature — making it suitable for everything from cryogenic applications to high-temperature aerospace actuators.
