05 — SDGs

Smart Materials
for a Better World.

The United Nations' 17 Sustainable Development Goals define humanity's roadmap to 2030. Shape Memory Alloys, through their unique properties and applications, make measurable contributions to four of them — improving health outcomes, enabling resilient infrastructure, and fighting climate change.

The UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), adopted in 2015, are a universal call to action to end poverty, protect the planet, and ensure prosperity for all by 2030. Materials science sits at the intersection of multiple goals — the right material in the right application can save lives, reduce emissions, and build cities that survive disasters. Nitinol and other SMAs are among the most cross-cutting materials in this regard. Their medical, structural, and energy applications directly advance four SDGs with documented, quantified impact.

3 Good Health

SDG 3 — Good Health & Well-Being

Saving Lives Through Smart Implants

SDG 3 calls for universal health coverage and access to safe, effective medical technologies. Nitinol-based medical devices directly extend this goal — enabling minimally invasive procedures that save lives, reduce recovery times, and lower healthcare costs in both developed and developing health systems.

🫀

Cardiovascular Disease Intervention

Cardiovascular disease is the world's leading cause of death — 17.9 million deaths per year (WHO, 2023). Nitinol stents used in percutaneous coronary and peripheral vascular interventions have reduced 30-day mortality for carotid stenosis procedures by over 30% compared to open surgery. Each year, 1.5 million+ patients receive Nitinol implants for cardiovascular conditions.

🧠

Stroke Treatment & Thrombectomy

Nitinol thrombectomy devices (clot retrievers) have transformed ischaemic stroke treatment. Clinical trials show 90-day functional independence rates improving from 26% (medical management alone) to 46% with mechanical thrombectomy using SMA devices. Stroke is the second largest cause of disability globally — these devices directly address SDG target 3.4 (reduce premature non-communicable disease mortality).

🦷

Accessible Orthodontic Care

Nitinol archwires reduce the number of orthodontic appointments required by 30–40% compared to steel wire treatment — a critical advantage in regions with sparse specialist coverage. Lower-cost treatment protocols become viable, contributing to SDG 3.8 (universal health coverage) by extending access to oral health interventions across lower-income settings.

🔬

Minimally Invasive Surgery

SMA guidewires, retrieval devices, and snares enable procedures that would previously require open surgery — reducing hospital stays from 7–10 days to 24 hours, cutting infection risk by up to 60%, and dramatically lowering procedure costs. This directly supports SDG 3.b (access to medicines and technologies in developing countries) by making complex interventions viable at lower resource levels.

Relevant SDG 3 Targets

3.4

Reduce premature mortality from non-communicable diseases by one third. Nitinol stents and thrombectomy devices directly reduce cardiovascular and stroke mortality.

3.8

Achieve universal health coverage. Lower-cost, less-invasive SMA-based procedures extend specialist-level care to under-resourced settings.

3.b

Support research and development of medicines and vaccines. Nitinol drug-eluting stents combine mechanical scaffolding with pharmaceutical delivery.

9 Innovation

SDG 9 — Industry, Innovation & Infrastructure

Engineering the Future with Smart Materials

SDG 9 calls for resilient infrastructure, inclusive industrialisation, and innovation. SMAs are enabling next-generation infrastructure that is lighter, stronger, and self-adaptive — from morphing aircraft wings to smart pipelines to seismically resilient bridges.

✈️

Aerospace Innovation

Boeing 737 MAX engine chevrons using Nitinol actuation reduce aircraft noise by 3 dB during takeoff — exceeding ICAO Stage 4 noise standards — while adding zero mechanical complexity. NASA's morphing wing research programmes estimate 5–8% fuel savings from SMA-enabled adaptive aerofoils. This directly supports SDG 9.4 (upgrade infrastructure for sustainability) by reducing aviation's environmental footprint.

🏗️

Resilient Infrastructure

SMA tendons and braces retrofitted into bridges and buildings provide passive seismic damping without maintenance. A 2019 study found SMA-braced frames reduced peak inter-storey drift by 45% compared to conventional steel bracing — with the additional benefit of self-centering after an earthquake. Contributes to SDG 9.1 (develop quality, reliable infrastructure).

🤖

Advanced Manufacturing

4D printing of SMA components — objects that change shape after manufacturing — is enabling entirely new product categories: self-assembling structures, reconfigurable tools, and adaptive packaging. The global SMA market's 13% CAGR reflects accelerating industrial adoption, contributing to SDG 9.2 (promote inclusive and sustainable industrialisation).

🔗

Pipeline & Connection Technology

Cryofit™ SMA pipe couplings have operated for 50+ years in military and commercial aircraft hydraulic systems with zero reported failures — a reliability record no conventional coupling technology matches. Extending this to oil and gas pipelines reduces maintenance costs and leak risk, supporting SDG 9.1's emphasis on reliable, sustainable infrastructure.

Relevant SDG 9 Targets

9.1

Develop reliable, sustainable infrastructure. SMA seismic dampers and pipe couplings create infrastructure that is self-maintaining and failure-resistant.

9.4

Upgrade infrastructure for sustainability. SMA-enabled aircraft noise reduction and fuel efficiency directly reduce aviation's environmental impact.

9.5

Enhance scientific research and technological capabilities. The $17.65B SMA industry funds ongoing research into new alloy compositions, training protocols, and applications.

11 Sustainable Cities

SDG 11 — Sustainable Cities & Communities

Cities That Bend But Don't Break

SDG 11 calls for cities that are safe, resilient, and sustainable. With 80% of the world's population projected to live in urban areas by 2050, and seismic risk affecting 130+ cities worldwide, SMA-based structural solutions offer a paradigm shift in how we protect the built environment.

🌉

Earthquake-Resilient Buildings

SMA braces allow buildings to deform during an earthquake — absorbing energy through the superelastic hysteresis loop — then return to their original geometry without human intervention. Post-earthquake inspections of SMA-braced structures in Japan and Italy have found zero residual drift after major events, versus 1–3% permanent deformation in conventional steel-braced frames.

🏛️

Heritage Preservation

Over 500 historic buildings in Italy, Turkey, and Greece have been retrofitted with SMA tendons since 2010. The technique preserves original masonry while providing seismic protection impossible with conventional steel reinforcement — which would require destroying historic fabric. Directly supports SDG 11.4 (protect cultural and natural heritage).

🚇

Transport Infrastructure

SMA dampers installed in elevated rail viaducts and metro systems in seismic zones (Tokyo, Los Angeles, Istanbul) allow service resumption hours after major earthquakes — versus weeks for conventionally designed structures. Uninterrupted urban transport is critical for post-disaster emergency response, supporting SDG 11.b (integrated policies for disaster risk reduction).

🏘️

Affordable Housing Resilience

Research programs in Chile, Nepal, and Peru are developing low-cost SMA connection systems for non-engineered masonry housing — the building type responsible for 80% of earthquake fatalities. Passive SMA-reinforced connections that require no maintenance or power are uniquely suited to informal settlement retrofitting, targeting SDG 11.5 (disaster risk reduction for the urban poor).

Relevant SDG 11 Targets

11.4

Protect cultural and natural heritage. SMA retrofits preserve historic buildings while providing modern seismic protection.

11.5

Reduce disaster deaths and economic losses. Self-centering SMA structures dramatically reduce post-earthquake repair costs and displacement of residents.

11.b

Implement integrated disaster risk reduction policies. Passive SMA dampers require no maintenance or power — ideal for resilience planning in resource-constrained cities.

13 Climate Action

SDG 13 — Climate Action

Low-Carbon Actuation for a Warming World

SDG 13 demands urgent action to combat climate change. SMAs contribute through passive actuation (zero electrical power required), enabling fuel-efficient aerospace systems, replacing energy-intensive hydraulics, and enabling renewable energy infrastructure that must operate in extreme conditions.

⚡

Passive Actuation — Zero Power

SMA actuators driven by ambient temperature change — such as thermal control valves in buildings, HVAC dampers, and greenhouse ventilation systems — consume zero electrical energy. A single SMA-actuated building ventilation damper can replace a 50W electric motor, operating continuously without power over its 10+ year lifetime. At scale across commercial buildings, this represents measurable carbon reduction.

✈️

Aviation Emissions Reduction

Aviation accounts for 2.5% of global CO₂ emissions. SMA-enabled morphing wings and adaptive engine components could reduce per-flight fuel consumption by 5–8% — equivalent to removing 35 million tonnes of CO₂ per year at current traffic levels. Boeing and Airbus both have active SMA integration programmes targeting their 2030 emissions commitments.

🌊

Offshore Wind & Tidal Energy

SMA components in offshore wind turbine blade pitch systems and tidal generator actuators function reliably in corrosive marine environments where conventional actuators require frequent replacement. Reducing offshore maintenance frequency by 40% — documented in North Sea pilot programs — directly reduces the carbon cost of renewable energy infrastructure.

🔋

SMA Thermal Energy Harvesting

Emerging research exploits the latent heat of SMA phase transformation to convert low-grade waste heat (50–150°C — from industrial processes, server farms, vehicle exhaust) into mechanical work or electricity. SMA heat engines with 3–8% conversion efficiency are being tested as distributed waste-heat recovery systems, directly targeting SDG 13.2 (integrate climate change into national policies).

Relevant SDG 13 Targets

13.2

Integrate climate change measures into policies and planning. Passive SMA actuators and thermal energy harvesters support decarbonisation strategies across industry, transport, and buildings.

13.3

Improve education and awareness on climate change mitigation. SMA research builds cross-disciplinary climate engineering capacity in materials science communities worldwide.
