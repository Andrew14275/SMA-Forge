03 — Applications

Where SMAs
Meet the World.

Sector Breakdown
🫀

Sector 01

Medical & Biomedical

The biocompatibility of Nitinol — near-identical elastic modulus to bone, corrosion resistance in bodily fluids, MRI compatibility — makes it the dominant SMA in medicine. Its ability to self-expand at body temperature (37°C) is uniquely exploited.

🩺

Cardiovascular Stents

Nitinol stents are compressed into a catheter in their martensitic state (cold, ~0°C), inserted into a blocked artery, then released. At body temperature (37°C) they self-expand to their memorised diameter, propping the artery open permanently. No mechanical deployment needed — the patient's own body heat activates the device.

● Self-expanding

Orthodontic Archwires

Nitinol archwires exert a continuous, gentle force on teeth throughout the day. Unlike steel wires that lose tension, Nitinol wires use their superelastic property to maintain constant pressure — reducing adjustment visits and accelerating treatment by up to 40%.

● Superelastic
🔬

Surgical Guidewires

Nitinol guidewires navigate tortuous vascular anatomy without kinking. They can be bent to near-180° and spring back perfectly — a failure mode that would be catastrophic with steel. Used in neurosurgery, cardiology, and minimally invasive procedures worldwide.

● Kink-resistant
🧠

Neurovascular Devices

Flow diverters and clot retrieval devices (thrombectomy stents) for stroke treatment. These mesh-like devices are delivered collapsed, then expand in the brain's blood vessels to capture or redirect blood flow — dramatically improving stroke survival outcomes.

● Life-saving
🦴

Bone Anchors & Staples

SMA staples used in spinal fusion and fracture repair clamp together bone segments with a force that self-adjusts as the bone heals and swells — preventing the fixation failure common with rigid titanium hardware.

● Adaptive fixation
🚀

Sector 02

Aerospace & Defence

Space and aviation demand lightweight, reliable actuation with zero mechanical complexity. SMAs replace hydraulic systems, motors, and gearboxes with a single alloy wire — reducing weight, failure points, and power consumption simultaneously.

🛸

Deployable Space Structures

NASA uses Nitinol actuators in spacecraft to deploy solar panels, antenna booms, and instrument arms after launch. The SMA is locked in its deformed (martensitic) state during launch, then heated by solar energy or electric current to deploy — no motors, no pyrotechnics, no moving mechanical parts that could jam in the vacuum of space.

● Zero-motor actuation

Adaptive Wing Geometry

Boeing and NASA DARPA research programs use SMA wires embedded in wing structures to morph aerofoil shape during flight — changing camber and twist to optimise lift and drag at different flight regimes, reducing fuel consumption by up to 5%.

● Morphing structures
🔩

Release Mechanisms

SMA release pins lock payloads, covers, and instruments during launch vibration, then release on command when heated. Replaces explosive bolts with a reusable, silent, and shock-free mechanism — critical for sensitive scientific instruments.

● Pyrotechnic-free
🌡️

Thermal Control Valves

Passively actuated valves that open or close based on temperature alone — no electronics, no power supply. Used in satellite thermal management systems and spacecraft radiators to regulate heat flow automatically across enormous temperature swings.

● Passive control
🛩️

Engine Noise Chevrons

Boeing 737 MAX uses Nitinol-actuated chevrons on the rear of jet engine nacelles. During takeoff (noisy, needs noise reduction) the SMA bends the chevrons inward; at cruise altitude (cold) they flatten out — optimising thrust. No actuators, no wiring — just temperature.

● Noise reduction
🤖

Sector 03

Robotics & Soft Actuators

SMA wires act as artificial muscles — contracting when heated, relaxing when cooled. This enables soft robots with no rigid joints, grippers that handle delicate objects without sensors, and bio-inspired locomotion at tiny scales.

💪

Artificial Muscles

SMA wire bundles produce contractions analogous to biological muscle — up to 150 MPa stress output from a 0.1mm wire. Used in prosthetic limb actuators, exoskeletons, and haptic interfaces. Far lighter than servo motors for equivalent force output.

● Bio-inspired
🪼

Soft Robotic Grippers

SMA-actuated fingers conform to object shape without force sensors or complex control algorithms. Used in food handling, surgical assistance, and fragile object manipulation. The alloy's compliance prevents damage to delicate payloads.

● Compliant grasping
🐛

Micro-locomotion

Millimetre-scale robots use SMA wires for crawling, swimming, and jumping locomotion. Applications include pipeline inspection, medical capsule endoscopy, and search-and-rescue in rubble where larger robots cannot fit.

● Miniaturised
🦾

Rehabilitation Exoskeletons

Wearable SMA actuator arrays assist stroke patients in finger and wrist rehabilitation. The soft, lightweight structure (vs. rigid motorised splints) allows natural movement patterns and can be worn for hours without fatigue or skin damage.

● Wearable therapy
📱

Sector 04

Consumer Electronics

As miniaturisation demands increase, motors and solenoids become too large and power-hungry. SMA micro-actuators fit inside smartphones, cameras, and wearables to deliver precise, silent movement at sub-millimetre scale.

📷

OIS Camera Actuators

Apple iPhone 14 Pro and later use SMA wire arrays for Optical Image Stabilisation — replacing voice coil motors with four SMA wires that shift the camera module in 2D. 40% smaller, 25% lower power, completely silent. A landmark adoption of SMAs in consumer hardware.

● Apple iPhone
🔭

Autofocus Mechanisms

SMA wire actuators drive lens elements in compact smartphone cameras with sub-micron precision. Faster response than piezoelectric systems, silent operation vs. voice coil motors, and no electromagnetic interference with wireless chips.

● Silent AF
⌚

Smart Eyewear Frames

Superelastic Nitinol frames for glasses can be twisted, sat upon, and fully deformed — then spring back perfectly. No permanent deformation possible within the superelastic regime. Luxottica and others have offered Nitinol frames since the 1990s.

● Superelastic
🏗️

Sector 05

Civil & Structural Engineering

At the macro scale, SMA cables and bars are embedded in bridges, buildings, and joints to provide passive damping during earthquakes and self-centering behaviour after seismic events — returning structures to plumb without human intervention.

🌉

Seismic Dampers

SMA braces installed in building frames absorb seismic energy through the superelastic hysteresis loop — converting deformation energy to heat rather than permanent structural damage. Demonstrated on bridges in Italy, Japan, and the USA.

● Earthquake-resistant
🏛️

Historic Building Retrofits

The San Giorgio church in Trignano, Italy (damaged in the 2012 Emilia earthquake) was retrofitted with SMA tendons that allow controlled lateral movement during seismic events while preserving the historic masonry — a technique now spreading across heritage sites.

● Heritage preservation
🔗

Self-Centering Connections

Beam-column connections fitted with SMA bolts deform under earthquake loading but return to their original geometry once the shaking stops. Buildings require minimal post-earthquake repair — a revolutionary shift from conventional "damage-tolerant" design.

● Post-quake recovery
🌡️

Pipeline Couplings

SMA pipe couplings are chilled, slid over pipe joints, then warm up to ambient temperature — contracting to create a grip stronger than welding. Used in aircraft hydraulic lines, oil pipelines, and submarine cable connectors since the 1970s.

● Cryofit™ joints
🌐

Emerging Frontiers: Current research is pushing SMAs into 4D-printed structures (objects that change shape after printing), soft robotics for deep-sea exploration, drug delivery micro-capsules that open at tumour temperature, and shape-morphing aircraft skins that adapt to weather in real time.
