02 — Phases

Two Phases.
One Memory.

Shape Memory Alloys exist in two distinct crystal structures — Martensite at low temperatures and Austenite at high temperatures. The reversible switch between them is what gives SMAs their extraordinary properties.

● Martensite

Martensite

Low Temperature Phase — Below Mf

The low-temperature phase, characterised by a monoclinic or orthorhombic crystal lattice. Atoms are arranged in a twinned structure that allows easy deformation. When stress is applied, twins re-orient — the alloy bends without breaking. Remove the stress: it stays deformed, but remembers where it came from.

Crystal Structure Monoclinic / Twinned
Stiffness Low (~28–41 GPa)
Deformability High — up to 8% strain
Yield Strength ~70–140 MPa
Appearance Soft, malleable
● Austenite

Austenite

High Temperature Phase — Above Af

The high-temperature phase, featuring a highly ordered cubic crystal lattice (B2 structure). Atoms snap back into their original, symmetrical positions. This is the "memorised" shape — the configuration the alloy was trained to return to. The structure is stronger and stiffer than martensite.

Crystal Structure Cubic (B2 ordered)
Stiffness High (~83 GPa)
Deformability Low — elastic only
Yield Strength ~195–690 MPa
Appearance Stiff, springy

Transformation Temperatures

The Four Critical Temperatures

Phase transformation in SMAs doesn't happen at a single point — it spans a temperature range defined by four transition markers. These can be tuned during alloy manufacturing to suit specific applications.

Ms

Martensite Start

~−20°C to 100°C

Cooling begins triggering martensite formation

Mf

Martensite Finish

~−50°C to 80°C

Full transformation to martensite complete

As

Austenite Start

~−10°C to 120°C

Heating begins triggering austenite formation

Af

Austenite Finish

~10°C to 150°C

Full recovery to memorised shape complete

🔁

Thermal Hysteresis: The heating and cooling curves don't overlap — this gap is called hysteresis. Transformation starts at different temperatures depending on direction (heating vs. cooling). For Nitinol, this window is typically 20–50°C wide, which engineers use to design predictable actuation behaviour.

Reference Temperatures — Nitinol
Symbol Name Typical Range (Nitinol) What Happens
Ms Martensite Start −20°C to +100°C Cooling initiates; first martensite domains nucleate
Mf Martensite Finish −50°C to +80°C Fully martensitic — alloy is soft and deformable
As Austenite Start −10°C to +120°C Heating begins reverse transformation; shape recovery starts
Af Austenite Finish +10°C to +150°C Fully austenitic — memorised shape fully recovered
🧪

Tunable by Composition: These temperatures are not fixed — they shift dramatically based on the Ni:Ti ratio and trace additions of Cu, Fe, or Co. A 1 at% change in nickel content can shift Af by up to 100°C, giving engineers precise control over actuation temperature.
